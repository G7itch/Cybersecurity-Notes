# Social Engineering

Social engineering is the act of manipulating people for advantage - often being detrimental to the victim. Social engineering works because people are vulnerable to manipulation and human beings are the weakest link in the security chain.

### Social Engineering Techniques

Social engineering attacks generally need two things:

1. The aim to achieve a goal of the attacker.

2. Emotional manipulation of the victim.

Phishing is a common attack with targets anyone with an email address and is malicious spam messages that aim to get at least one attack. Phishing is a broad terms and can encompass other techniques such as:

- **Spear-Phishing**: Adversary targets one individual or set of individuals.

- **Whaling**: Spear phishing against high-profile target in an organisation.

- **Vishing**: Voice phishing.

- **Smishing**: SMS phishing.

There are other types of social engineering attacks including:

- **Quid pro quo**: Adversary offers a service in return for privileged information.

- **Pretexting**: A situation devised to invoke an emotional response in the target.

- **Baiting**: An enticing situation that tempts a victim to act. Relies on positive emotions.

It is additionally possible for less intrusive social engineering attacks. These attacks may prompt the user to go the the attacker instead of the other way around. This include scareware attacks.

- **Honeypot traps**

- **Watering hole attacks**: Exploiting a well-frequented space by the victim.

- **Scareware**: Scares the victim into making rash decisions and going towards the attacker for help.

- **Tailgating**: Exploits social convention to gain access to a physically restricted area.

Most of the time, the tactics used by attackers will evolve over time. A crazy example of this is the *Emily Williams* attack.

### Insider Threats

Insider threats are threats that come from inside the organisation. This can include employees, contractors or business partner. There are different ways to categorise insider threats:

- **Negligent**: Users that do not realise they are helping a threat actor. Types include:

  - *Pawn*: Authorised user manipulated to help a bad actor, for example via phishing.

  - *Goof*: Deliberately takes careless behaviour but does not have malicious intent.

- **Malicious**: Actions could include espionage, fraud or IP theft. Types include:

  - *Mole*: An outsider who has gained access to the inside of the network by manipulation.

  - *Lone wolf*: Work independently and without outside influence.

  - *Collaborators*: Authorised third party who works with the business.

Insider attacks can be harder to detect than external attacks. However, there are behavioural indicators that can help. Specifically we are looking for individuals who are disgruntled or whose behaviour changes irrationally. Digital indicators also exist such as login times, volume of data transfer, internet searches or excessive asset requests.

To make organisations more secure it is important that you as an individual follow all of your organisations security policies.

Organisations should keep logs of all critical assets and rank them.

### Fraud, Scams and Influence Campaigns

**Cyber Fraud**: A Social engineering technique, malware or other type of deception used to defraud or take advantage of a person or organisation.

**Cyber Scam**: A type of fraud that are generally classified as petty or not as serious as cyber fraud.

Examples may include:

- Fake government websites.

- Dating and romance scams.

- Holiday fraud.

- Mandate fraud.

- Pharming.

- Greeting card scams.

**Influence campaigns**: Large-scale efforts to shift public opinion. Such campaigns are generally executed in bad faith. Usually carried out by groups with high-levels of capability.
